The agent

Mara Delgado answers her own phone.

Mara has walked these blocks daily since 2012, first as the neighbor with the fast dog, now as the agent who knows which houses hide knob-and-tube wiring and which merely hide good bones. Her buyers tour the neighborhood on foot before they ever tour a house.

She works alone, on purpose, with one licensed assistant and a list of tradespeople earned across 300 closings. Clients get her cell number, not a team routing line, and the advice she would give her own sister. Twice a year, that advice is: do not buy this one.

Working with Mara

Whichever side of the door you're on.

Buying

Find it without losing your head.

  1. Walk first

    Before any showing, we walk two neighborhoods with coffee in hand. You learn what a fair price looks like from the sidewalk, before a staged kitchen can argue with you.

  2. See it clearly

    On tours I point at the roof, the panel, and the sewer line while everyone else admires the styling. You will know what the house is, not what it is dressed as.

  3. Win it calmly

    An offer built from what actually closed on that street, terms a seller takes seriously, and a steady voice from inspection to keys.

Selling

Sell the street, then the house.

  1. Price from evidence

    Comps I have stood inside, not an algorithm's guess from three towns over. We set a number the market recognizes on day one.

  2. Prepare what pays

    Two weekends of the right work. My crew list, my stager, and nothing that will not return its cost at the closing table.

  3. Tell it properly

    Photography at golden hour, copy that sells your block as hard as your kitchen, and a median of nine days to contract.

Field notes

Rowan Park, annotated.

No. 01 · Coffee

The coffee census

Fern & Filter on Bellamy takes the quiet 7am crowd; the porch at Odd Hours on Carver collects the strollers by nine. If your offer needs writing on a Saturday, I am at the corner table at Fern's.

No. 02 · Schools

The catchment line

Rowan Park Elementary's boundary runs down the middle of Alder Street. The east side walks, the west side drives, and the line moves prices by roughly four percent. I map it on every tour.

No. 03 · Streets

Streets to watch

Hawthorne holds its craftsman stock like a museum. Bellamy's brick rows trade fast and quietly, often before the sign goes up. And Carver, the mid-century strip everyone overlooked for a decade, is having its moment.
